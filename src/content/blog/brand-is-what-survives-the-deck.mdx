Twenty years in, I've watched a lot of brand work get made and remade. Most of it doesn't survive the deck it was born in.

The reason is simple. A brand is not an asset package. It's the thing that's left in someone's head ninety seconds after they close the tab, put down the pitch, walk out of the room. If nothing is left – no feeling, no association, no sense of what you'd do and wouldn't – you don't have a brand yet. You have a style guide.

## The test

I've stolen this test from a founder I worked with years ago and never given it back. Here it is.

Describe your brand in one sentence to someone who has never heard of you. Make them repeat it back to you a day later, from memory.

The gap between what you said and what they remember is the brand. Not the words themselves – the shape of the idea that stuck. If they remember nothing, the brand is nothing. If they remember something slightly wrong but interesting, you're close. If they remember it cleanly, you're done. The rest is execution.

## What most companies mistake for brand

Logos. Typography. The color blue, but a slightly different blue. A voice document that reads like a personality test from a magazine.

None of those things are wrong. They just aren't the brand. They're the clothes the brand shows up in. A person with no personality does not become interesting by changing their outfit.

The brand is the personality. It's the point of view. It's the thing you believe about the world that most of your competitors don't, said clearly enough that people can repeat it.

## Where the work actually starts

It starts with the founder's conviction. What do you actually believe that's contrarian? What are you willing to be wrong about in public? What would you refuse to do even if it printed money?

Those answers are the raw material. Everything downstream – the identity, the voice, the visual system – is just translation work. And translation work done on top of nothing produces nothing.

That's why brand projects that start with a moodboard tend to end with a moodboard.
